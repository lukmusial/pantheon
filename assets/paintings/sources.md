# Pompeian / Herculanean wall-painting references: sources

Reference photos for composing Pompeian-style painted wall panels in the non-commercial Unreal Engine
reconstruction of the Pantheon interior (c. 126 AD). All files are in `raw/` and were downloaded from
Wikimedia Commons via the API (thumbnail URLs, long side capped near 4000 px; files whose original is
under the cap, or where Commons serves no smaller standard thumbnail, are stored at original size).
Licence and author are copied from each file page's metadata (`LicenseShortName`, `Artist`).

"MANN" = Museo Archeologico Nazionale di Napoli. Regio/insula/door numbers follow the Fiorelli system.
Style = Pompeian style (First to Fourth) as given on the file page (ArchaiOptix entries) or the usual
attribution for the house/room; "(not stated)" marks my attribution where the file page is silent.

## Notes and uncertainties

- Status: complete. 21 mythological panels plus 15 decorative/lararium/border images; 36 files, about 264 MB.
  Every file was checked with `file` and `sips` and is a valid JPEG. I looked at all of them as previews
  or full images to confirm the subject, a frontal view and no bad cropping.
- **Size cap:** Commons now serves thumbnails only in fixed width steps (e.g. 3840 px). For portrait images
  narrower than 3840 px, the API returns the original file, so several are stored above 4000 px on the
  long side: 9559 (4816), 114321 (4961), 9529 (5109), 9551 (4565), Ceii lararium (5120), Louvre Apollo (4938).
  The files were not resized locally. Downscale them in the texture pipeline if needed.
- **Below the 4000 px cap but above 2000 px:** Apollo and Daphne MANN 9535 (2504 px) is the smallest file.
  Nothing better was found for this scene from Pompeii or Herculaneum. That panel is from **Stabiae** (Villa
  Arianna), not Pompeii or Herculaneum.
- **Licences:** 32 files are CC BY-SA 4.0 (attribution + share-alike), one is CC BY 4.0
  (Lucretius Fronto Mars and Venus), and one is CC BY-SA 2.0 (Carole Raddato, House of the Prince of Naples).
  The Met's Boscotrecase Black Room photo is CC0. `deco_third_style_red_tablinum_house_of_lucretius_fronto.jpg`
  is tagged PD-Art on Commons but is a **scan from a 2005 book** (M. Ranieri Panetta, ed., *Pompeji*, Belser,
  p. 254). That depends on the PD-Art reasoning (a faithful photo of a 2D work) and on Italian heritage-image
  rules, so treat it as a lower-confidence licence. ArchaiOptix pages also note that the MANN permits photos
  for private, educational and scientific use; check this if the project ever becomes commercial.
- **Uncertain attributions:** The Pompeian style of the Herculaneum Basilica/Augusteum panels (9049, 9109,
  9008), the Ceii lararium and Apollo and Daphne 9535 is my attribution, not the file page's. MANN 8905
  is "third style (?)" on its page, and its findspot is only "Regio VIII, insula 2 or 3". ArchaiOptix gives
  the findspot of MANN 9701 as "IV, 1, 7", which is almost certainly a typo for VI 1, 7 (House of the Vestals).
  MANN 9413 (Actaeon) is given as "Herculaneum or rather Portici". MANN 9506 (Icarus) and the Venus in the
  shell from VII 6, 7 have no house name, and the Venus has no inventory number. The Isis-portico frieze
  has no inventory number. The House of Mars and Venus VII 9, 47 is called the "house of the wedding of
  Herakles" on its file page.
- **Not Third/Fourth style but kept as requested:** the Villa of the Mysteries red ground and the MANN 9901
  garland panel (House of the Scientists) are Second style. The Samnite House wall is First style (stucco
  imitating marble blocks).
- **Not found at good resolution (skipped):** Neptune/Poseidon and Amymone (only 19th-c. drawings and a
  Gragnano/Stabiae triclinium); Medea from Herculaneum MANN 8976 (only a 1751 px-wide, not frontal museum
  shot), so Medea MANN 114321 from Pompeii was used instead; Narcissus as a separate panel (the Narcissus
  picture is in `deco_fourth_style_red_yellow_wall_narcissus_house_of_vestals_MANN9701.jpg`); Venus Pescatrice;
  panels centred on Ceres/Demeter or Minerva (Achilles restrained by Athena MANN 9104 exists but is
  fragmentary). The in-situ Venus in the Shell
  (II 3, 3) photos found showed the wrong wall, so the MANN replica-type panel from VII 6, 7 was used. No good
  photo of a painted street/compitum altar was found: the street-shrine photos on Commons are scaffolded or
  faded. Painted serpents with an altar are covered by MANN 8905 and MANN 112286.
- The Ixion and Pentheus rooms (House of the Vettii) and the Augustales wall are in-situ room photos, so
  they have some perspective and, for Ixion/Pentheus, slight wide-angle distortion. Rectify them before use
  as texture references.
