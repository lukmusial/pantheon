# Attic openings and coffer centres: evidence and recommendation

Compiled 2026-11-21 for the c. 126 AD reconstruction. Builds on `reference/dimensions.md` sections 6, 7 and 13.

Tags: **[E]** evidence (ancient remains, measured surveys made before 1747, or peer-reviewed scholarship
read this session); **[R]** Renaissance or later restoration drawing (a conjecture, but made while the
original attic still stood); **[S]** my own informed reconstruction, i.e. speculation.

## 1. What the attic openings are

| Fact | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 14 openings, one on each of the 16 attic axes except the entrance and apse arches | [E] | Desgodetz 1682 pl. 18; Hannah & Magli 2009; `dimensions.md` §6 |
| The 6 over the lateral exedrae are real openings. They do not open to the outside: they pass through into the upper part of the exedra recess (behind the main entablature), so they carry light *from the rotunda into the exedrae* | [E] | Desgodetz; `dimensions.md` §4 "rises behind the entablature up to the attic windows, which light it"; guide literature describes "a small window in each alcove" |

| Opening c. 2.05 wide x 3.1 high, sill on the attic dado (c. 15.1 m above the floor); framed by white marble (pavonazzetto per F. Piranesi) frames with a flat cornice | [E]/[S] scaled | F. Piranesi Tav. XII, scaled |
| Depth of the blind niches | not found | — |

Scholarship:

- Thomas 2017 (*JRS*, "The cult statues of the Pantheon"): "there were actually fourteen of these openings
  and ... it is not clear that they were intended for statuary." He does not count them among his 34 statue
  places. [E] https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/6F33641FC3985AA73E86F505477AA64F/core-reader
- No ancient text mentions the attic openings. No fixing holes for statues or grilles in the blind niches are
  reported in any source I could read (De Fine Licht 1968 and Loerke 1990 could not be opened).

**Conclusion on evidence:** nothing proves what, if anything, stood in the blind niches. The earliest
records (Raphael, Desgodetz) show them empty; the one restorer who put statues in them (Palladio, followed
by Ligorio's count) also invented statues for every other niche. Thomas doubts they were meant for statues.

| Evidence | Tag | Source (via `dimensions.md` §13) |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing survives. Mortise holes, three square holes below each coffer, T-shaped clamps in the coffer centres | [E] | De Fine Licht 1968 p. 146, via Brackett 2004 pp. 65-66 |

| Desgodetz: the stucco had fallen; "reason to believe" in gilt-bronze roses | [E]/[R] | Desgodetz 1682 |

## 4. Recommendation

### 4.1 Six real attic windows (over the E, W and four diagonal exedrae)

- **[S]** Close each with a **gilded-bronze lattice grille** (transenna) set flush in the opening, square or
  lozenge lattice c. 0.20 m mesh, bars c. 25 mm, matching the bronze transom grille over the door
  (`dimensions.md` §9). The 1930s marble scale-pattern transennae are a defensible alternative; either reads
  as a grille from the floor. No glazing.
- Behind the grille: the upper exedra space, dim, catching a little oculus light; in the night/fire scenario a
  faint warm glow from the exedra's own lamps below (see `lighting.md`).

### 4.2 Eight blind niches (over the aediculae)

Options, in order of evidence:

| Option | Evidence | Visual effect |
|---|---|---|
| A. Empty recess, back wall in revetment (e.g. pavonazzetto or a porphyry panel framed in serpentine) | Raphael, Desgodetz, F. Piranesi show them empty | Calm, correct, but the user saw them as "left empty" |
| B. Small standing statue, c. 2.0-2.2 m including a 0.25 m plinth, painted marble or gilded bronze | Palladio 1570 drawing; Ligorio's count of 44 | Most "Roman" look; rhymes with the aediculae below |
| C. Painted panel (Pompeian mythological picture) on the back of the niche | none for the Pantheon | Reads as a framed picture; at 15-18 m height and 20 m distance only colour and broad figures read |

**Recommended [S]: option B**, labelled as a Renaissance-attested restoration, not evidence:

- One figure per blind niche, about 2/3 of the niche height (2.0-2.2 m figure on a 0.25 m plinth in a
  3.1 m opening), frontal, painted marble with the same subtle polychromy as the lower statues
  (`polychromy.md`), so they read as a second, lesser register.
- Subject **[S]**: the gods not yet represented below, plus personifications, chosen so each sits over the
  aedicula of a related god. Suggested set: Juno, Vesta, Vulcan, Latona, Victoria (x2, flanking the apse arch,
  holding wreaths toward the apse), and the Dioscuri (Castor and Pollux, flanking the entrance arch). The
  Victories flanking the apse also echo F. Piranesi's caryatids on the apse columns.
- If scans are lacking: use smaller-scale Victories / generic draped female figures (Vesta and Juno types are
  common draped matrons and can share one scan with different paint). `assets/statues/manifest.md` has Juno
  and Vulcan as heads only, plus a full draped Livia-as-Fortuna and a draped Aesculapius from Chiragan, which
  could serve (Livia body for Juno/Vesta; Aesculapius is itself a fitting god for a niche).
- Lighting: the blind niches get only bounced daylight; no lamps at 15 m (nothing to stand on, no access).
- If the scene budget is tight, fall back to option A with a porphyry panel at the back, which is the
  evidence-safe choice. Option C (paintings in the attic) is the weakest; put paintings lower where they can be
  read (see `paintings_plan.md`).

### 4.3 Coffer centres

**Recommended [S], following MacDonald / Brackett:**

- A **gilded bronze rosette** (eight- or sixteen-petalled acanthus rosette, double whorl, central boss) fixed
  at the centre of the back panel of every coffer. Diameter about 0.30 of the back-panel width:
  ring 1 c. 0.75 m, ring 2 c. 0.70, ring 3 c. 0.60, ring 4 c. 0.50, ring 5 c. 0.42 m; relief c. 0.10-0.15 m.
- Coffer steps: white stucco with small egg-and-dart (ovolo) and bead-and-reel mouldings on the step edges,
  in white or very lightly gilded.
- Ribs ("bands round the coffers", painted per F. Piranesi): a soft warm white with a thin painted line
  border; do **not** paint a dark blue starry field. The blue-with-stars scheme (Thomas's "plausibly") is
  a valid alternative if the art direction wants it, but it has no physical evidence and would darken the
  dome badly in the render.
  **Adopted 2026-11-24 (user's art direction): the dome is painted blue as the night sky**, lighter on the
  ribs and deepest in the coffers, with the gilded rosettes as its stars (`/Game/Materials/M_DomeSky`,
  PLAN.md D1). It remains a conjecture, not a finding.
- Smooth zone above ring 5 up to the oculus: plain white stucco; optionally the bronze plating Ziolkowski
  suggests, as a narrow gilded-bronze band just below the oculus cornice.
