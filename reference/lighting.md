# Lighting by fire in a Hadrianic temple interior (c. 126 AD)

Compiled 2026-11-21. Purpose: replace or supplement the purely daylight setup with the fire sources a Roman
temple of the early 2nd century AD would actually have had, with numbers usable in Unreal.

Tags: **[E]** evidence (ancient text, excavated object, or scholarship read this session, with URL);
**[U]** published figure I could not open or verify myself; **[S]** my estimate or reconstruction
(speculation). Images referred to are in `reference/images/lighting/` (see the table at the end).

---

## 1. What the evidence says, in short

1. **Daylight did the main work.** Roman temples were lit through the open doors (Vitruvius 4.5.1 wants the
   cult statue to face the door so worshippers at the altar outside face it [E]). In the Pantheon the oculus is
   the main source: on a sunny day it admits several million lumens, and no amount of lamps competes with
   that (section 5). Fire inside is accent light: visible flames and warm pools in the darker exedrae,
   niches and the apse.
2. **Lamps and lamp-stands were standard temple furniture and gifts.**
   - Pliny, *Natural History* 34.14, praises lamp-stands "hanging in temples" (*lychnuchi pendentes in
     delubris*) or shaped like trees with lamps for fruit, such as the one in the temple of **Apollo on the
     Palatine** that Alexander had taken at Thebes [E as text; wording quoted from memory of the Latin —
     check at https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D34%3Achapter%3D8].
   - Cicero, *Verrines* 2.4.60-71: a jewelled candelabrum that King Antiochus intended to dedicate to
     **Jupiter Capitolinus** (cited by Smith, *Dictionary*, "Candelabrum") [E]
     https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/secondary/SMIGRA*/Candelabrum.html
   - Smith: large **marble** candelabra "stood in temples and palaces", fixed to the ground [E].
   - Pausanias 1.26.6-7: the golden lamp of Callimachus before Athena Polias at Athens, filled once a year,
     burning "both day and night", with an asbestos wick and a bronze palm tree above it to carry off the smoke
     [E] https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0525/tlg001/perseus-eng2/1.26.1-1.27.1 . A Greek precedent
     for a perpetual lamp before a cult image.
   - Seneca, *Letters* 95.47, mocks lighting lamps as an act of worship ("the gods do not need light, and
     even men take no pleasure in soot") — proof the practice was common [E]
     https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_95
   - Excavated sanctuaries yield lamps inside the cella: e.g. the temple of Allat at Palmyra, lamps clustered in
     two groups inside the cella [E] https://pam-journal.pl/article/137053/en
3. **Blood sacrifice happened outside.** The main altar (*ara*) stood in front of the temple, in the open,
   facing east, lower than the cult statue (Vitruvius 4.9) [E]
   https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/4*.html . Inside the cella offerings were
   **incense and wine**, burnt on a small altar, a portable brazier (*foculus*) or a tripod in front of the
   image. Vitruvius 4.9 also speaks of altars "in the middle of temples" (*aedibus mediis*) and wants those of
   Jupiter and the celestial gods "as high as may conveniently be" [E].
4. **The Pantheon itself:** no ancient altar is known inside the rotunda. Thomas 2017 notes the present apse
   altar is later and that placing altars in front of the images is Nissen's 1906 conjecture [E]
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/6F33641FC3985AA73E86F505477AA64F/core-reader . Hadrian also used the
   building to hold court (Cassius Dio 69.7.1) [E]. So: small incense altars, tripods and lamp-stands before
   the statues are **reconstruction [S]**, consistent with general temple practice.
5. **"Fire pits"**: open fire pits in a marble temple floor are **not attested** in Roman temples. The Roman
   equivalents are the **bronze brazier** (*foculus*, *foculare*: a metal tray or box of glowing charcoal on
   legs), the **tripod** (bronze stand carrying a bowl), and the **altar with a fire-tray** on its top. Use
   these wherever the brief says "fire pit". [S, based on E above]
6. **Torches** (*faces*: pine splints, tow soaked in pitch or resin) were for processions, night rites and
   streets, not steady interior light [E general; Smith "Fax"]. One or two in iron holders at the door at
   most [S].

## 4. Placement for this building [S]

Layout reference: apse S with Jupiter (7 m) on a 2 m pedestal; six exedrae with statues; eight aediculae;
entrance N. Counts below give about **50 flames**, which reads as richly furnished without clutter.

| Location | What to place | Count | Position | Light |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Apse (Jupiter)** | 2 Hadrianic marble candelabra (Barberini type) with fire bowls | 2 | either side of the pedestal, 0.8 m in front of its face, 2.2 m off axis | 2 x 800 lm, 1800 K, at 2.6 m |
| | 1 marble altar with incense fire | 1 | on axis, 2.5 m in front of the pedestal (Vitruvius: lower than the statue, "as high as convenient" for Jupiter: 1.2-1.3 m) | 500 lm at 1.4 m |
| | 2 bronze tripod braziers (charcoal + incense) | 2 | at the apse mouth, beside the projecting columns | 2 x 200 lm at 1.0 m |
| **Each of 6 exedrae** | 2 bronze candelabra with 2-wick lamps | 12 | in front of the statue base(s), 0.6 m forward, 1.5 m apart (lateral exedrae with 3 niches: between the niches) | 25 lm each at 1.35 m |
| | 1 bronze tripod brazier or small altar with incense | 6 | on the exedra axis, 1.5 m in front of the statue | 150-250 lm at 1.0 m |
| **Each of 8 aediculae** | 1 bronze lamp on each column pedestal (the original pedestals were separate blocks under each column, `dimensions.md` §5), or 2 small candelabra | 16 | flanking the statue, at pedestal top (c. 2.1 m) | 10-20 lm each |

Smoke: one thin incense column per brazier/altar, rising and spreading under the entablature; in the
oculus shaft it would add to the volumetric haze already in the scene.

## 5. Daylight versus fire: what will show in the render [S]

- Oculus: c. 54-62 m² of opening. With the sun in it (c. 100,000 lux) it admits about 5-6 million lumens, plus
  skylight. Average illuminance on the floor and walls is of the order of hundreds to a few thousand lux.
- A 20 lm lamp gives about 2 lux at 1 m and 0.2 lux at 3 m. Even all 50 flames together add only a few
  thousand lumens, i.e. well under 1 % of the daylight.
- Consequence: with the present exposure (+5 EV inside) the flames will read as small bright points and only
  the statue faces and bases within 1-2 m of a flame will show a warm tint. That is historically right.
- If the brief wants fire to visibly model the statues, options: (a) a **late-afternoon / dusk** variant in
  which the sun is out of the oculus and interior exposure rises by 3-4 EV; (b) keep physical values but add a
  per-light "artistic gain" of 3-5x only for lights inside exedrae, aediculae and apse, and say so in the
  credits; (c) a night ceremony variant with only fire light (exposure about EV 3-4 at ISO 100).
