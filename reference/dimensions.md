# Pantheon (Rome) — reference dimensions for the c. 126 AD reconstruction

All values in metres unless stated. Compiled 2026-11-21.

## How to read this file

Confidence tags used in every table:

- **[P]** published figure, read in a source this session (URL given).
- **[D]** converted from Desgodetz's 1682 measured survey (English ed. 1771), which was made *before* the
  1747 attic demolition. Paris foot = 0.32484 m, inch = 0.02707 m.
- **[S]** scaled by me from an engraving in `reference/images/` (Francesco Piranesi 1787-90 plates),
  calibrated on a published dimension. Expect +/- 3-5 %. Treat as modelling guidance, not survey data.
- **[O]** observed in a photograph in `reference/images/`.
- **[U]** unverified: from secondary/popular sources only, or from memory of the literature. Check before relying on it.

Units: Roman foot (RF) = 0.2956-0.2958 m (Pelletti; De Fine Licht, quoted in Brackett 2004 p. 61 n. 6).
Palmo romano (Piranesi plates) = 0.2234 m.

Source abbreviations (full list at the end): **Moore** = romanconcrete.com; **Desg** = Desgodetz;
**Martines** = Marder & Wilson Jones 2015 ch. 4; **Brackett** = UGA thesis 2004 (quotes De Fine Licht 1968,
Loerke 1990, Wilson Jones 2000); **H&M** = Hannah & Magli 2009; **Thomas** = Thomas 2017 (Journal of Roman Studies);
**FP** = Francesco Piranesi plates; **it/en/de.wiki** = Wikipedia.

Sources I could NOT open (paywall / server error), so their numbers appear only as quoted by others:
De Fine Licht 1968 *The Rotunda in Rome*; Wilson Jones 2000 *Principles of Roman Architecture*; Loerke 1990
(JSAH); the Bern Digital Pantheon repository (repository.edition-topoi.org/collection/BDPP returned a database error).

### Entrance bay (N)

Same arch form as the apse: barrel vault breaking into the attic, no columns. The vault springs from the main
cornice; it oversails the wall by c. 1.0 (3 ft 1.5 in) (Desg). [D]

## 6. Attic zone — ORIGINAL (pre-1747) design

History: the antique revetment survived until Paolo Posi stripped it for Benedict XIV (sources give 1747, 1753 or
"for the 1750 Holy Year") and substituted the present stucco scheme of pedimented blind windows and square panels.
A short stretch was rebuilt to the antique design under Alberto Terenzio in 1929-34 (Loerke 1990 via search
abstract; it.wiki says "1930s, not entirely accurately").

Evidence used: Desgodetz 1682 pl. 18 text (measured the original); F. Piranesi 1787 Tav. XII with legend;
Raphael drawing c. 1506, Palladio 1570, Panini c. 1734 (all in `reference/images/`); the Terenzio bay.

| Item | Value | Tag / source |
|---|---|---|
| Height range | 13.05 - 21.72 above floor (8.67) | derived |
| Sub-zones, bottom to top | (a) plain dado/pedestal band c. **2.05**; (b) pilaster order incl. socle, base, capital c. **4.85**; (c) attic entablature (architrave + frieze + springing cornice) c. **1.75** | [S] FP Tav. XII and FP section agree |
| Relief | **None.** Pilaster shafts, panels and architrave fasciae are flush with the wall, distinguished only by stone colour; bases and capitals are in very low relief | [D] pl. 18 |
| Rhythm | 16 equal units of 22.5 deg (8.53 m at the wall). Each unit = 1 window bay + 3 panel bays, divided by **4 pilasters** | [S], [O] restored bay |
| Bay widths (pilaster axis to axis) | window bay c. 2.9; panel bays c. 1.85 / 2.0 / 1.85 | [S] |
| Pilasters | porphyry shafts, c. 0.37 wide, c. 4.85 high with base and capital (very slender). Nominal count 16 x 4 = **64**, less those cut by the apse and entrance arches | [D], [S]; count [U] |
| Windows | **14** (16 axes minus entrance and apse). The 6 over the lateral exedrae are real openings lighting the exedrae; the 8 over the piers/aediculae are blind, sunk "like a square niche" | [D]; H&M |
| Window opening | c. 2.05 wide x 3.1 high, sill on the dado; with frame c. 2.95 wide; cornice on consoles above | [S] |
| Window grilles | The restored bay has pierced marble transennae (scale pattern); antique evidence for these is unknown to me | [O], [U] |
| Alignment | Windows sit on the 16 axes (over exedra centres and over aediculae). Attic pilasters do NOT align with the columns below or the 28 dome ribs | Brackett p. 71 (Pelletti); Loerke |

Materials of the original attic:

| Element | Desgodetz 1682 (pl. 18) | F. Piranesi 1787 (Tav. XII legend) |
|---|---|---|
| Dado ("basement") | white marble | pavonazzetto, with white marble base and cap |
| Pilaster shafts | porphyry | porphyry |
| Pilaster bases/capitals, socles | white marble | giallo antico (reading uncertain) on a pavonazzetto socle with a porphyry astragal |
| Field / framing bands | white marble field; serpentine borders | bands of pavonazzetto and of serpentine |
| Large panels in the two outer panel bays | "isabella" (yellowish = giallo antico) with serpentine border; small serpentine panels above and below | giallo antico |
| Middle panel bay | large panel with isabella border; porphyry roundels above and below, flanked by small upright serpentine panels | porphyry and pavonazzetto panels; giallo roundels set in pavonazzetto squares |
| Over each window | small square panels (isabella in serpentine border) and a porphyry roundel; porphyry frieze between window jamb and cornice | porphyry roundels; window frames and cornices pavonazzetto, their frieze serpentine |
| Architrave | three flush bands: white / serpentine / white, white cymatium | pavonazzetto / serpentine / pavonazzetto |
| Frieze | — | giallo antico (but Wilson Jones and the building today show a **porphyry** frieze band) |
| Cornice (springing) | white marble, survives | white marble, "still existing" |

Recommended palette: porphyry pilasters and roundels, giallo antico main panels, serpentine (green) borders and
middle architrave band, pavonazzetto/white field, dado pavonazzetto, porphyry frieze, white cornice.

### Restored sample bay (Terenzio, 1929-34)

Location: SW sector, directly above the SW diagonal exedra, i.e. the first bay to the right of the apse as seen
from the door ([O] `photo_attic_restored_bay_in_context_right_of_apse.jpg`).
What it shows ([O] `photo_attic_restored_original_bay_1930s.jpg`): a plain veined-marble dado about a quarter of
the attic height; two windows with white marble frames, flat cornices and pierced marble grilles; between them
four dark-red porphyry pilasters with small white capitals; three panel bays, each a stack of small panel / tall
panel / small panel, the middle bay with roundels top and bottom; three small panels (square, roundel, square)
over each window; green (serpentine) borders; a red porphyry frieze band under the white springing cornice.

## 14. Statues

Ancient texts:

- Cassius Dio 53.27.2-4 (on Agrippa's building): it held images of many gods "including Mars and Venus";
  Agrippa wished to place Augustus there, was refused, set a statue of the deified Julius Caesar inside and
  statues of Augustus and himself in the pronaos.
- Pliny, Natural History: book 9 (the Venus in the Pantheon wore Cleopatra's pearl as earrings); 36.38
  (caryatids by Diogenes of Athens "on the columns" and pediment statues); book 34 (Syracusan bronze
  capitals). All refer to the Agrippan building. Passages as quoted in en.wiki and it.wiki.
- No ancient source lists the statues of the Hadrianic rotunda.

Scholarly reconstructions (none is evidence-based for individual niches):

| Proposal | Content |
|---|---|
| Mommsen 1867 | The seven exedrae held the seven planetary gods (Sol, Luna, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn). Widely repeated, pure hypothesis |
| Nissen 1906 | Mars and Venus together in the apse; Divus Julius in the first exedra right of the door; Jupiter, Juno, Apollo, Diana, Neptune, Minerva etc. elsewhere |
| F. Piranesi 1787 | "Jupiter Ultor" in the apse, caryatids over the apse columns, statues in every aedicula and exedra niche (see Tav. XII) |
| Godfrey & Hemsoll 1986 | The apse was an imperial tribunal, not a statue setting |
| Thomas 2017 | Apse: **Mars, Venus and Divus Julius** as a triad. E and W exedrae: triads built on Apollo-Diana(-Latona) and Jupiter-Juno(-Cybele). Diagonal exedrae: pairs such as Vulcan-Vesta, Neptune-Minerva, Mercury-Ceres. Eight aediculae: single figures. 34 statue places in all: 6 niches in the two curved exedrae, 20 in the four diagonal ones, 8 aediculae |

Modelling suggestion: apse triad after Thomas, single over-life-size figures in the aediculae, three figures in
each lateral exedra's back niches; label all as conjecture.

## 15. Numbers I could not verify

- Exedra depths (4.0-4.9 spread) and exact exedra widths: only scaled from engravings.
- All aedicula dimensions: scaled from two F. Piranesi plates.
- Attic sub-zone heights, bay widths, window size, and the "64 pilasters" count: scaled / inferred.
- Coffer ring angles: scaled from one 1790 engraving (+/- 1.5 deg); individual step widths and depths other
  than the 0.73 total; whether upper rings are shallower.
- Portico entablature height, pediment pitch and apex height; split of base vs capital heights.
- Transitional block depth.
- Floor: drain-hole count (22) and the "89 squares" come from Wikipedia/guide pages; the two camber accounts conflict.
- Oculus: the spread 8.2-9.45 is real disagreement about what is measured (bronze rim, brick ring, outer lip).
- Stone names for wall-panel ground and for the triangular aedicula columns; material of the exedra antae.
- Whether attic windows had grilles in antiquity.
