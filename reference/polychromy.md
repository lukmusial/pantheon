## 1. What is known

### 1.1 General principle

- Greek and Roman marble sculpture was painted; white marble was meant to be seen, but as a *ground*.
  The best-documented Roman case, the **Augustus of Prima Porta** (Vatican), had paint on "the clothing, on
  details of the armor, on the hair and on details of the eyes but **not on the skin or on the ground of the
  armor**", which "maintain the color and transparency of the Parian marble" (Liverani, Vatican Museums
  analyses from 2002) [E] http://www.digitalsculpture.org/augustus/liverani.html
- Other portraits *did* carry flesh paint. The **Copenhagen Caligula** (Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek IN 2687,
  37-41 AD): red/pinkish areas on the skin, red ochre directly on the marble, Egyptian blue particles in skin
  and hair; black (bone black) on eyebrows, lashes and left eye; "two small grains of blue" in the right iris;
  "pale pink ... on the rims and inner corners of the eyes"; pink madder between the lips; hair red, yellow
  and black; binder egg [E] https://trackingcolour.com/objects/146 . Several other Glyptotek Roman heads
  (IN 821, 822, 761, 1435) show flesh tones built from red ochre, carbon black and Egyptian blue, and hair in
  yellow and red ochre, golden-reddish [E] https://www.trackingcolour.com/objects/128 (and neighbouring
  object pages).
- Practical reading [S]: both practices coexisted. For a Hadrianic temple interior use **a thin warm glaze on
  skin** (ganosis, see below) rather than opaque flesh paint, and keep all colour concentrated in eyes, lips,
  hair, drapery, borders and attributes. That matches the Prima Porta result and still reads as "painted".

### 1.2 Skin: ganosis

- Vitruvius 7.9.3-4: cinnabar walls are protected by rubbing on Punic wax melted with oil, warming it with
  coals and polishing it with a candle and clean cloths, "as is done with nude marble statues"; the Greek
  name is *ganosis* (Gwilt's translation renders it *kausis*) [E]
  https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/7*.html
- Pliny, *NH* 35.133: Praxiteles valued most those of his statues "to which Nicias had set his hand"
  (*circumlitio*, the painting/finishing of the surface) [E as text, quoted from memory].
- Effect for rendering [S]: wax with a little pigment gives the marble a warm, slightly translucent, satin
  sheen (roughness 0.25-0.35 on skin vs 0.4-0.5 on drapery), a faint ivory-peach tint, and deepens the
  subsurface look. Female skin lighter and cooler; male skin warmer and darker (the Greek and Roman
  convention seen in painting and on painted sculpture).

### 1.4 Eyes

- Painted, not inlaid, on marble statues of this date (Hadrianic sculptors only began to *carve* iris and pupil
  in the 130s) [S, standard art-historical dating].
- Iris: a ring of colour, typically brown, red-brown or grey-blue; the Caligula iris has blue grains [E].
- Pupil: black dot, with a small unpainted (marble) highlight sometimes left [S].
- Upper lid: a fine dark line (black or dark brown) with short lashes; lower lid lighter; pink at the rims and
  the inner corners (caruncle) [E Caligula].
- Eye white: left as waxed marble or very faintly warmed [S].
- Eyebrows: fine dark strokes following the carved ridge [E Caligula].

### 1.5 Drapery and borders

- Garments in solid colours, often light (white, saffron, pale blue) with **coloured borders**: the Pompeii
  Artemis (Naples, inv. 6008) wears a red under-blouse, a white dress and white mantle "decorated with bands of
  pink at the edges"; light brown pigment between the toes; pigments include Egyptian blue, pink madder,
  cinnabar, red iron oxide, haematite, lead yellow, yellow ochre, kaolin with lead white [E]
  https://maam.comune.grosseto.it/?p=3648 ; Liebieghaus reconstruction
  https://liebieghaus.de/de/polychromie/artemis-pompeii
- Borders and hems: bands 2-6 cm wide in a contrasting colour, often with a painted meander, wave
  (running-spiral), egg-and-dart, or ivy/vine scroll in gold or a second colour [S, after Brinkmann's
  reconstructions and Pompeian painting].
- Prima Porta: tunic and cloak bright madder red; on the cuirass, blue for metal objects (Mars's helmet, a
  chariot wheel, the standard), blue then later yellow (to suggest gold) for the fringe of the lappets [E].

## 2. Global settings for the scene [S]

Use a **marble base material** (white Luna/Pentelic, subsurface on) and a **paint layer** blended over it by
masks. Keep the paint thin: the marble crystal sparkle and translucency should still show through in skin,
and only opaque in hair, borders and attributes.

| Layer | Colour (sRGB hex) | Opacity over marble | Roughness | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Skin glaze, female | #EDD5BF | 25-35 % | 0.30 | cheeks and lips get extra pink |
| Skin glaze, youthful male | #E3C3A2 | 30-40 % | 0.30 | — |
| Skin glaze, mature male | #D9B38E | 35-45 % | 0.32 | — |
| Skin glaze, Hercules / Vulcan (sun-tanned, labouring) | #CF9F78 | 45-55 % | 0.35 | — |
| Cheek / ear / knuckle blush | #D99A8A | 10-15 % | — | soft gradient |
| Lips | #B5655C (female), #A55E50 (male) | 60-75 % | 0.35 | madder; Caligula evidence |
| Eye rims and inner corners | #C98F84 | 50 % | — | Caligula evidence |
| Pupil | #15110F | 100 % | 0.2 | wet look: roughness 0.2 |
| Upper lid line and lashes | #2B1E17 | 90 % | — | lines 1-2 mm at life size |
| Eyebrows | #3A2A20 (dark-haired), #6B4A2E (fair) | 70 % | — | fine strokes |
| Gold leaf | #D4AF37 base, metallic 1, roughness 0.2 | 100 % | — | on bolsters, borders, attributes |
| Gilded bronze (separate attributes) | #C9A04A metallic 1, roughness 0.25 | — | — | thunderbolts, sceptres, tridents |

Rules of thumb:
- Drapery saturation at most about 55-65 % (HSV S) and value 35-80 %; no pure primaries.
- Borders 1/30-1/20 of figure height wide; one ornament repeat per 8-15 cm at life size.
- Hair: base colour over the whole mass, darker in the drill channels, lighter or gilded on the crests of
  locks.

## 4. Altars and statue bases

Evidence [E]:
- Marble altars of the imperial period are carved with garlands, bucrania, ribbons, paterae and jugs, and
  carried paint like the statues; the Ara Pacis (Rome, 9 BC) preserves microscopic paint traces, and was shown
  with projected colour in 2009-2016 [U — studies not opened this session].
- Inscriptions on bases and altars were picked out in red (minium/cinnabar) letters [E general Roman practice; U
  for specific Pantheon bases].
- Pompeian **lararia** and street altars are masonry, stuccoed and painted: red or yellow grounds, garlands,
  sacrificial scenes, serpents (agathodaimones) approaching an altar, imitation marble [E; images in
  `assets/paintings/raw/`, see `assets/paintings/sources.md`].

Recommendation for the marble altars and bases in this scene [S]:

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Altar body | waxed marble ground #ECE4D6 |
| Mouldings (base and crown) | egg-and-dart and bead-and-reel picked out: eggs ivory, darts and shells red #A3443C, beads gilded; cyma faces with a thin blue band #3D5FA0 |
| Garlands in relief | leaves green earth #55703E with lighter tips, fruit coloured (pomegranates red, apples yellow, grapes purple), ribbons (*taeniae*) madder red #B23A3A fluttering |
| Bucrania | bone ivory with dark eye sockets; fillets red and white |
| Bolsters (*pulvini*) | scale or leaf pattern gilded; end rosettes gilded |
| Fire-tray on top | bronze, soot-darkened at the centre |
| Inscription | letters painted red #9C2F27 |
| Altar faces without relief | a framed painted panel (Pompeian style): red ground with a sacrificial scene or attributes of the god (thunderbolt and eagle for Jupiter etc.), see `paintings_plan.md` |
| Statue bases | revetted in coloured stone matching the room (porphyry or giallo antico panel in a white marble frame) with a gilded-bronze or red-painted inscription; crown moulding as altars |
