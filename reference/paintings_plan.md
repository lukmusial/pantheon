# Painted decoration plan: where mythological paintings go, and how to compose them

Compiled 2026-11-21. Source images: `assets/paintings/raw/`, catalogued in `assets/paintings/sources.md`.
Related: `polychromy.md` (statues, altars, bases), `lighting.md`, `attic_niches.md`.

Tags: **[E]** evidence; **[S]** speculation / informed reconstruction.

## 1. What the evidence allows

- **The Pantheon's lower walls were coloured marble, not painted plaster.** Desgodetz (1682) recorded the
  panelling beside the aediculae as a dark marble ground with giallo-type panels and porphyry roundels; the
  attic was marble too (`dimensions.md` §5-6, 12) [E]. No ancient source mentions paintings in the Pantheon.
  Painting the marble walls over would contradict the evidence.
- **But Roman temples did display paintings.** Famous Greek panel pictures (*tabulae*, on wood, in frames)
  were dedicated in Roman temples: Augustus placed Apelles' *Venus Anadyomene* in the temple of the Deified
  Julius (Pliny, *NH* 35.91); Zeuxis' *Marsyas bound* hung in the temple of Concord (Pliny 35.66); Agrippa
  himself bought paintings for public display and argued that all pictures should be public (Pliny 35.26)
  [E as texts. 35.91 checked: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_Anadyomene ("dedicated by the late
  Augustus ... in the shrine of Caesar his adoptive father"; replaced under Nero by a copy by Dorotheus).
  35.66 and 35.26 cited from memory of book 35; check them before quoting on screen]. The
  Pompeian and Herculaneum mythological panels are largely *copies and adaptations* of such Greek panel
  paintings, which is why they are the right style source.
- **Unknown finishes** that could legitimately be painted: the upper back walls of the exedrae (F. Piranesi
  shows three relief panels over the niches of the lateral exedrae), the backs of the statue niches, the apse
  semi-dome (original finish unknown), altars and statue bases [E that they are unknown; S that they were
  painted].
- **Period style.** By 126 AD the "Fourth Style" of Pompeii (62-79 AD) had evolved, but Hadrianic paintings at
  Ostia, Rome and Hadrian's Villa still use red, yellow and black panel systems, thin architectural frames
  and central picture panels, moving toward lighter grounds and a linear framework [E general; details S].
  Example: Ostia, Insula of the Paintings, built under Hadrian, has painted aediculae on red and yellow grounds
  in corridor 29-30 and room 33; red and yellow ochres dominate Ostian painting; white grounds with thin red
  line frames appear on Roman vaults from the Golden House onward [E]
  https://www.ostia-antica.org/regio1/4/4-2.htm .
  Composing in a refined Third/Fourth Style idiom is therefore defensible.

## 2. Proposed placement [S]

Four kinds of painting, from most to least defensible:

1. **Framed panel pictures (*tabulae*)** hung on, or set into, the marble walls: wooden panels in gilded
   frames, the way famous pictures were displayed in temples. They sit on the pier walls either side of each
   aedicula, in the zone between the aedicula pedestal top (c. 2.2 m) and the main entablature, so the
   marble revetment stays visible around them.
2. **Painted back walls in the exedrae**, above and around the statue niches (the zone F. Piranesi gives to
   relief panels), as a continuous Fourth-Style wall system with a central mythological picture.
3. **Painted grounds inside the statue niches and aediculae**: a deep red or black ground with fine
   candelabrum-and-garland ornament behind each statue, so the painted statue stands out.
4. **Painted altar faces and statue-base panels** in the lararium manner (see `polychromy.md` §4).

The apse semi-dome: keep F. Piranesi's lozenge coffers with gilded rosettes (stucco, white with gilding);
optionally a deep blue ground in the lozenges. No figural painting there.

## 4. Style guide for composing the panel textures [S, from the reference images]

### 4.1 Ground colours

| Name | sRGB hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Pompeian red (cinnabar/red ochre) | #9B2A1F (cinnabar) / #8E3524 (red ochre) | main panels, exedra fields |
| Pompeian yellow (yellow ochre) | #C8962E | alternating panels, frames |
| Black | #1C1A17 (slightly warm) | dado, panels behind statues, Third Style fields |
| White / cream | #E8E0CC | upper zones, Hadrianic light grounds |
| Green earth / malachite | #4E6B4A / #2F7A5A | thin frame lines, garlands |
| Egyptian blue | #3D5FA0 | sparing: sky in pictures, small bands |
| Purple-red (haematite / *purpurissum*) | #6E2C3A | dado, sub-frames |
| Gold (yellow ochre highlight / leaf) | #D2A64A | candelabra, border details |

Values are for the dry, lime-bound paint, matte (roughness 0.6-0.75); polished *encaustic* areas on the
best walls had a slight sheen (roughness 0.45).

### 4.2 Wall system (exedra back walls)

Tripartite vertically:

1. **Dado** (*zoccolo*) 0.9-1.2 m: black or purple-red with sprouting plants or painted marble imitation
   (veined panels like giallo and pavonazzetto — "opus sectile in paint"), matching the real stones nearby.
2. **Main zone** 60 % of the wall height: a large central panel (red or yellow) with the **picture in its
   centre**; flanked by narrower side panels in the alternate colour or black, each with a small floating
   figure or vignette; panels separated by narrow black or white architectural vistas with thin columns,
   or by slender **candelabra** (vegetal stems with discs and small figures) in yellow on black.
3. **Upper zone** 20-25 %: white or cream ground with delicate painted architecture (aediculae seen in
   perspective, garlands, small figures), lighter and airier than the main zone.

Frieze between main and upper zone: 15-25 cm band, black ground with a running ornament (palmette, tendril,
animals and Cupids) in yellow, white and green.

### 4.3 Frames and borders

- Central pictures: framed by a 3-6 cm ensemble, from outside in: dark line, a light (white or yellow) band,
  a thin red line, then the picture. Some pictures sit in a painted aedicula (columns and pediment) instead.
- Panel borders: the Fourth Style "embroidery border" (*Stickereiborte*): an openwork lace-like band 3-5 cm
  wide of tiny repeated motifs (palmettes, lotus, dots) in white/yellow/green on the panel ground, set 6-10 cm
  inside the panel edge.
- Thin lines: white or yellow 4-8 mm lines doubling the borders.

### 4.4 Proportions

- Main-zone panels: width : height about 1 : 1.2 to 1 : 1.5.
- Central picture: 1/3-1/2 of the panel width, nearly square (1 : 1 to 4 : 5), its centre slightly above the
  panel centre.
- Figures in the pictures: 2/3-3/4 of picture height for the main figures; landscape or architecture
  background in muted blue-green and ochre; strong light from one side; loose, fast brushwork with white
  highlights.
- *Tabulae* (hung panel pictures): picture 4 : 5, frame gilded bronze-coloured wood 1/12 of the shorter side,
  a thin red inner fillet.

### 4.5 Composing the textures from the photos

1. Crop each source picture to its painted frame; correct perspective; neutralise colour casts with the
   museum white balance (Naples photos often run warm under tungsten) to the palette above.
2. **Restore, don't copy damage**: fill losses and cracks (content-aware fill / inpainting), but keep the
   brushwork; boost saturation and value slightly toward fresh lime paint (+10-15 % saturation; lift the
   blacks from the ash-darkened greys).
3. Build each wall field at 1 px = 2 mm (a 4 x 3 m exedra wall = 2000 x 1500 px; a *tabula* 1.3 x 1.65 m =
   650 x 825 px minimum, better 2 px per mm for close-ups).
4. Compose: ground colour + border ornament (procedural or traced from `deco_*` images) + the restored
   picture + soft painted shadows; add very subtle plaster roughness in the normal map.
5. Texture sets per wall: base colour, roughness (0.6-0.75), low-strength normal (plaster ripples and
   brush ridges).
6. Lighting check: under the fire-light (1800-1900 K) reds and yellows warm strongly and blues go dark; test
   panels under the daylight and the fire setups (`lighting.md`) before finalising.
